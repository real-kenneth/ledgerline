Each budget line gets its own forecast method, because dues collected in January, an insurance premium not yet billed, and a monthly electric bill should not be projected the same way. Ledgerline flags material variances, reconciles to the source workbook, bridges to year-end operating cash, and drafts next year's budget.

          Methods and assumptions

          • Forecast methods. Run rate: actual ÷ share of year elapsed. Full-year amount recognized: the year's revenue or expense is already on the books, so the forecast equals the actual (this says nothing about whether the cash has been collected). Scheduled payment and estimate: an entered full-year amount, with the reason in the note column.
          • Share of year elapsed is computed from the actual cutoff date (), not from month-end.
          • Two different measures. "% of budget used" compares spending to date with the full-year budget. "Forecast vs budget" compares the expected full-year total with the budget.
          • Status thresholds (5% watch, 15% over, plus the dollar materiality threshold) are configurable management screens, not industry standards.
          • Unbudgeted lines (budget of zero with activity) are flagged as unbudgeted rather than divided by zero. Assessment discounts are contra-revenue: they reduce revenue, are shown as not separately budgeted, and never receive a favorable flag.
          • Cash scenarios. Accounting forecasts become cash only through stated assumptions: the share of remaining revenue received, interest deposited to operating accounts, receivables collected (0%, 50%, 100%), liabilities settled, and transfers to reserves. Each result is shown beside its assumptions.
          • Reserve timing. Costs are inflated from the cost-estimate year to the replacement year. Component age comes from the separate valuation year, so updating a quote does not change how worn an asset is. Year-1 contributions are the entered amount; growth starts in year 2. Interest is earned on the opening balance each year, which can overstate interest when a large replacement happens early in a year.
          • Minimum contributions are threshold-funding results, not full funding. Ledgerline reports two: the smallest year-1 contribution meeting every year-end balance, and the smallest meeting a replacement-first low point (the year's replacements paid from the opening balance before contributions and interest arrive). The low point is a timing stress test, separate from the annual interest approximation.
          • Source checks compare every line's budget and actual against an unchangeable copy of the source data, keyed by stable line IDs (not general-ledger account numbers). All agreement checks use one tolerance: amounts agree when they differ by less than one cent.
          • Input validation. Percentages are limited to 0–100. Calendar years and useful lives must be whole numbers (lives at least 1); an invalid entry is outlined and blocks the reserve calculation until fixed, so no replacement is ever silently dropped.
          • Percent-funded bands (under 30% weak, 30% to under 70% fair, 70% and above strong) follow common practitioner guidelines. They are not a CAI-mandated grade, and 70% is not fully funded.
